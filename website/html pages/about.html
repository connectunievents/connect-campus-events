Our story

Why Connect exists

As a committee member of my university's music club, I saw the same problem play out event after event: we'd pour hours into marketing, chasing down group chats, posting flyers, messaging people individually — just to get students to notice something was even happening. On top of that, finding sponsors willing to support student events was its own uphill battle, with no real starting point beyond cold outreach and guesswork.

Connect started as a response to that frustration. Instead of every club, planner, and sponsor working in isolation across scattered pages and group chats, Connect brings them onto one shared platform — a single place to discover what's happening across campuses, and a bridge that connects events, sponsors, and the people organizing it all.

Mission

What we're building toward.

Connect's mission is simple: make campus life easier to find and easier to support. For students, that means one trusted place to discover what's happening — public events open to anyone, and private, university-only events reserved for verified students. For event planners and clubs, it means less time spent on manual outreach and more time spent building great events. And for sponsors, it means a clear, direct way to find and support the student communities they want to reach — instead of relying on cold outreach and guesswork.

What sets us apart

Built differently.

Most event pages are either fully public or fully closed — Connect is built around access that actually reflects campus life. Verified students unlock private, university-only events alongside everything public, while general users can still browse and register for open events without needing a university email at all.

Every registration comes with an instant QR code ticket — no printing, no manual check-in lists, no guesswork at the door. And on the sponsorship side, businesses can apply directly through the platform, with each application reviewed before going live, so students only ever see sponsors who are genuinely part of campus life. Underneath it all, access control isn't just a frontend rule — it's enforced at the database level, so private events stay private no matter how someone tries to reach them.
